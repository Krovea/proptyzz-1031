How a 1031 exchange works

Section 1031 of the tax code lets you sell business or investment real estate and reinvest the proceeds in other "like-kind" real estate without paying capital gains tax on the sale at that time. The tax is deferred, not forgiven.

1

Plan before you list

The exchange has to be set up before the sale closes. Once you take the money yourself, it is too late.

2

Use a qualified intermediary

A neutral third party holds the sale proceeds and buys the replacement property for you, as the rules require.

3

Meet both deadlines

45 days to identify, 180 days to close. Missing either one generally means the gain becomes taxable.

You don't have to become a landlord again

Many owners who have managed property for decades want to defer the tax but are done with tenants, repairs and late-night calls. Replacement property doesn't have to be another building you run yourself.

Owning and managing directly

  • Full control over the property you buy
  • You handle tenants, upkeep and financing
  • Finding a suitable property inside 45 days can be hard

Passive ownership options

  • Some structures qualify as like-kind but are run by a professional sponsor
  • No day-to-day management on your side
  • They carry their own risks and suit some owners, not all; a licensed representative can explain the details

Common questions

What counts as "like-kind" property?

For real estate, it is broad: most real property held for investment or business use can be exchanged for other real property held the same way. A rental house can go into an apartment building, land, or commercial property. Your home and property held mainly to resell don't qualify.

Do I have to reinvest all of the money?

To defer all of the gain, you generally need to reinvest all of the net proceeds and replace any debt that was paid off. Cash you keep, often called "boot", is usually taxable.

What happens if I miss the 45-day deadline?

The deadlines are not extended for weekends or holidays, and generally can't be extended at all. If you don't identify replacement property in time, the exchange usually fails and the gain becomes taxable in the year of the sale.

Is it too late if my property is already listed?

No. What matters is setting up the exchange with a qualified intermediary before the sale closes. If you're under contract, it's worth talking it through soon.
