Why owners get caught out

Most 1031 problems come from timing, not from the rules themselves.

01

The clock starts at closing

You have 45 calendar days to identify replacement property in writing, weekends and holidays included. Most owners start looking far too late.

02

The money can't touch your hands

Sale proceeds have to go to a qualified intermediary. If the exchange isn't set up before closing, it can't be fixed afterwards.

03

Debt has to be replaced too

If your property carried a mortgage, you generally need to replace that debt as well as reinvest the cash, or the shortfall is taxable.

Common questions

What counts as "like-kind" property?

For real estate it is broad: most property held for investment or business use can be exchanged for other real property held the same way. A rental house can go into an apartment building, land or commercial property. Your own home and property held mainly to resell don't qualify.

Do I have to reinvest all of the money?

To defer all of the gain you generally need to reinvest all of the net proceeds and replace any debt that was paid off. Cash you keep, often called "boot", is usually taxable.

Is it too late if my property is already listed or under contract?

No. What matters is setting up the exchange with a qualified intermediary before the sale closes. If you're under contract, it's worth talking it through soon.

What if I'm already inside my 45 days?

Then the identification deadline is the priority. Some professionally managed options can be identified and closed quickly because the property is already acquired and financed. A short call helps you see what's realistic in the time left.
